Features

Native, frugal, self-contained.

Written in Swift and SwiftUI. A 2.6 MB menu bar agent with no runtime dependencies beyond macOS itself.

Neural Engine inference

WhisperKit through CoreML, running on Apple Silicon's dedicated hardware rather than burning a CPU core or a datacentre GPU.

Nothing leaves your Mac

No audio, transcript or usage data is ever transmitted. Recordings exist in memory for the length of a transcription and are then gone.

Types where your cursor is

Inserts at the caret through the macOS Accessibility APIs, so it works in Safari, Slack, VS Code, Notes, Terminal and everything else.

Cleanup you control

Filler words, self-corrections and punctuation are handled by rules you can switch off one by one. Nothing is rewritten behind your back.

Your choice of hotkey

Push-to-talk on fn, right ⌥ or the dictation key. Or toggle with fn+Space. Double-press for hands-free.

Local history & dictionary

Search everything you have dictated and re-insert it. Teach LocalFlow the names and jargon it should always spell your way.

FAQ

Questions worth asking.

Does audio really never leave my Mac?

Yes. There is no networking code in the transcription path. LocalFlow touches the internet exactly twice: when you download a speech model, and when you buy Pro. Pull the network cable and everything else keeps working — including your Pro license, which is verified offline.

What is the catch with the free version?

There isn't one. Free is unlimited dictation with the full rule-based cleanup and two of the three speech models. No word caps, no trial period, no nag screens. Pro adds the local language model polish, the most accurate Whisper tier and per-app styles.

The source is public. Can't I just remove the license check?

Technically, yes — and that is the price of publishing the source. Legally, no: LocalFlow is under the Elastic License 2.0, which allows you to read, fork, modify and build it, but not to remove or work around the license key. Practically: it is maintained by one person, and €15 once is what makes that sustainable. If you genuinely cannot afford it, use the free version with a clear conscience — it is a real product, not crippleware.

Which languages does it handle?

Whisper's multilingual models, so most major languages. The cleanup rules are tuned for German and English. Auto-detect copes well with mixing the two mid-sentence; naming a language explicitly helps on very short phrases.

Will macOS warn me when I open it?

No. Releases are signed with a Developer ID certificate and notarised by Apple, so LocalFlow opens like any other app — no warning, no right-click trick. If you would rather not trust a prebuilt binary, it compiles from source with one script.

Does it work on Intel Macs?

It runs, using the whisper.cpp backend instead of the Neural Engine. Expect noticeably higher latency than the sub-second figures here, which are measured on Apple Silicon.

Why does it need Accessibility access?

It is the only supported way for a macOS app to type into another app's text field. LocalFlow uses it to insert at your cursor and — if you leave the option on — to read the few characters before the cursor so it can match capitalisation. That context is never stored and never read from password fields.

I lost my license key.

It is in your purchase confirmation email from Stripe. The success page link in that email also still works — open it again and the key is there. Failing that, open an issue with your order number.
